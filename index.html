FRAME 01
EXPOSURE

Three controls.
Three tradeoffs.

Aperture, shutter speed and ISO all influence brightness, but each also changes something else in the photograph. That “something else” is where creative control begins.

Aperture

A wider opening lets in more light and can reduce depth of field. A narrower opening can keep more depth sharp, depending on distance and focal length.

Shutter speed

Faster speeds reduce visible subject or camera motion. Slower speeds allow more motion blur and gather light for longer.

ISO

Higher ISO can make a given exposure practical in lower light, usually with a tradeoff in image noise and tonal quality.

FRAME 03
FOCUS

Sharpness has
more than one cause.

A soft photo can come from missed focus, subject motion, camera movement, shallow depth of field, lens behavior or processing. Diagnose the type of softness before changing everything.

Check where detail is sharp.

If something in front of or behind your subject is sharper, focus placement may be the issue.

Look at motion direction.

Streaking or directional softness can point toward subject or camera movement.

Consider depth of field.

Close distance, longer focal lengths and wider apertures can make the sharp zone feel very thin.

FRAME 04
LIGHT

Look before
you meter.

Photography is easier when you first notice the direction, size, contrast and color of the light.

Direction

Front, side and back light reveal shape differently.

Hardness

A small apparent light source tends to make harder-edged shadows; a larger apparent source tends to soften them.

Color

Daylight, shade and artificial sources can have different color characteristics. White balance controls how the camera interprets them.

FRAME 05
COMPOSITION

Make the frame
say one thing.

Composition is less about obeying a grid and more about deciding what deserves attention—and what does not.

Choose the subject.

Be specific about what the photograph is actually about.

Check the edges.

Distracting bright patches, cut-off objects and background tangencies often hide near the frame edge.

Change your position.

Moving yourself can change foreground, background, overlap and perspective more meaningfully than zooming alone.

Simplify.

If an element does not support the subject, consider excluding it through position, timing or framing.

FRAME 06
QUICK ANSWERS

Questions photographers actually ask.

What is the exposure triangle?

It is a teaching model connecting aperture, shutter speed and ISO. All three affect how an image is rendered, but they do not have identical physical roles: aperture and shutter alter how much light reaches the sensor, while ISO changes the camera system's response/output brightness and comes with quality tradeoffs.

Why are my photos blurry?

Common possibilities include missed focus, subject movement, camera shake or insufficient depth of field. Zoom into the image and identify what kind of softness you see before choosing a fix.

Should I always use the lowest ISO?

Not necessarily. A low ISO can be useful for image quality when enough light and an appropriate shutter/aperture are available. Raising ISO can be the better tradeoff when it enables the shutter speed or aperture your photograph needs.

What does aperture change besides brightness?

Aperture influences depth of field and can also affect lens rendering. The visible result depends on focal length, focus distance, subject-background distance and the lens itself.

RAW or JPEG—which should I use?

RAW generally preserves more capture data and editing flexibility, while JPEG is processed in-camera and convenient to share. The useful choice depends on your workflow, storage and editing needs.

Do I need manual mode to learn photography?

No. Aperture priority, shutter priority and other modes can be excellent learning tools. Understanding what the camera is prioritizing matters more than using one particular mode.

ABOUT

FIELD SAFETY

Protect your eyes,
gear and subjects.

Never look directly at the sun through an optical viewfinder, telescope or other magnifying optical system without appropriate purpose-built solar protection. Be mindful of privacy, permissions and local rules when photographing people or restricted locations. For equipment-specific warnings, follow the manufacturer documentation.
